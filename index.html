Coquitlam, rising.

A Vancouver suburb, raised one building at a time.

Coquitlam is a city of about 150,000 people in Metro Vancouver, roughly 20 km east of downtown Vancouver. Long a suburb of detached homes and green space, it is one of the region's fast-growing cities, and since the Evergreen SkyTrain line opened in 2016, high-rises have been climbing around City Centre and Burquitlam.

Day 5 / Sight

An exploration of place through building height.

Built on open data

Building footprints and road centrelines: City of Coquitlam Open Data ↗. Supplemental names and addresses: the supplied Statistics Canada building dataset.

Read the map with care

Heights are recorded values, shown without vertical exaggeration on flat ground. The Height unknown filter shows missing or zero heights as grey footprints. This is an exploration of the supplied data, not a verified current inventory.

About the data & how to read this project

Coverage and completeness

The map includes all 33,770 building/structure footprints and 3,536 road segments in the supplied city files. Of those footprints, 30,751 have positive heights; 2,714 have no height and 305 have a zero value. Missing and zero heights are hidden during the reveal and appear as flat grey shapes only when the Height unknown filter is selected. No heights are estimated.

Footprints are source features, not necessarily unique buildings. The layer includes structures such as canopies and tanks. One anomalous category label is suppressed while its footprint remains included.

Height and perspective

The city's HEIGHT field controls extrusion. The slider sets a reveal threshold: footprints with positive recorded heights at or below that value appear in 3D at their full height. Taller buildings remain completely hidden until the slider reaches them. At 124 metres, all usable heights are revealed. Colour and chart counts always represent recorded heights, regardless of the slider setting or zoom.

The city geometry is supplied in WGS84. Original shapes are retained with coordinate rounding; terrain and roof details are not modelled. Perspective affects apparent size. The overview fits the supplied footprint extent, not a municipal boundary.

Names, addresses and dates

All 24 names and 8 of 9 addresses from the Statistics Canada file were retained through unique spatial matches with at least 95% footprint overlap (one malformed address was left out, and phone numbers were removed from two names). A named facility may occupy only part of a larger building: the City Centre library name, for example, sits on a 105 m tower footprint. Most footprints have no street address; road address ranges have not been assigned to buildings.

The building file contains no construction years or observation date, so this map shows the city's shape as recorded, not its growth over time; the context in the introduction comes from outside the data. A few records look odd (one staircase is listed at 45.6 m) and are shown as supplied. Road construction dates describe roads, not buildings.

Reading the numbers

The height chart uses all 33,770 mapped footprints as their denominator, including those with unavailable heights. Of these, 26,636 (78.87%) are under 10 metres. The median of 7.01 metres excludes missing and zero values; the maximum recorded height is 123.10 metres.
